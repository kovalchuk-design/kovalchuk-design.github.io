Terry Costa · via NewDay agency · Dallas, TX

A compare view for dresses that all look the same

Redesigning a prom and formal retailer’s storefront so near-identical dresses could be judged side by side, instead of across ten open tabs.

01 · The problemTerry Costa · e-commerce storefront

Results

3-way compare, shipped

Shoppers can set two or three near-identical dresses side by side instead of opening tabs.

Cart that never leaves the page

An in-page drawer replaced the old route to a separate cart screen.

1 system, every template

Category, PDP, brand story, and editorial content all read as one storefront.

Situation

Terry Costa sells thousands of prom and formal styles, each in half a dozen colors and ten-plus sizes — visually, a wall of near-identical Sherri Hill gowns. The old storefront treated every dress as a standalone page with no way to compare two side by side.

A shopper deciding between two similar necklines or two shades of blue had exactly one tool: opening the same site in a second tab.

Deciding insight

Session recordings showed shoppers repeatedly opening the same product twice in separate tabs just to hold two dresses next to each other — the missing feature wasn’t better photography, it was a compare view.

That reframed the PDP work: build the comparison the shopper was already improvising, instead of polishing a single-dress page further.

Research

Cross-referenced repeat-product-view patterns against how competing formalwear retailers handled comparison, and settled on a lightweight compare table — color, size, fabric, neckline — that a shopper could build from any product page in one click.

02 · What I didStorefront, remote agency work

Approach

Built the compare table, a cleaner PDP with size guide and color swatches, and an in-page cart drawer that kept shoppers from ever routing to a separate cart page.

Rolled the boutique’s black-square wordmark and pink accent consistently from category pages through the brand story, best-sellers rail, and blog editorial.
